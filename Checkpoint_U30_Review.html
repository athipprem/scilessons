🧮 Priority Refresher — Combining THREE Forces

The rule: when THREE (or more) forces act on something, don't just add up every number you see. First, SORT the forces into two groups by direction — everything pushing/pulling ONE way in one group, everything pushing/pulling the OTHER way in the other group. Second, COMBINE each group (add same-direction forces together). Third, SUBTRACT the smaller group's total from the bigger group's total — that leftover is the real net force, and it moves toward the bigger group's direction.

On Unit 29's test, a question with two forces pushing forward and one pushing backward was missed — all three numbers were added together as if they all pushed the same way, instead of grouping by direction first. Let's lock in the 3-step sort-combine-subtract process before the checkpoint test. Full lesson →

Quick Check 1: Three dogs pull on the same sled. Two dogs pull LEFT with 15N and 10N. One dog pulls RIGHT with 8N. What is the net force, and which way does the sled move?

✔ Correct — Step 1: group by direction (LEFT: 15+10=25N; RIGHT: 8N). Step 2: subtract the smaller group from the bigger group: 25N − 8N = 17N. Step 3: it moves toward the bigger group's direction — LEFT.
✘ First group the forces by direction (LEFT: 15+10=25N; RIGHT: 8N), THEN subtract: 25N − 8N = 17N, moving left — the bigger group's direction.

Quick Check 2: What is the very FIRST step you should take when a problem gives you three or more forces to combine?

✔ Correct — always sort by direction FIRST. Only after the forces are grouped should you combine (add) each group, then subtract the smaller group's total from the bigger one.
✘ Direction always comes first — sort the forces into "same direction" groups before doing any adding or subtracting.
