Quick Recap 🔁

From Lesson 1: flowering plants reproduce SEXUALLY from SEEDS — pollination moves pollen from an anther to a stigma, then fertilisation joins the pollen with the ovule, forming a seed. Ferns and mosses instead reproduce from SPORES — tiny cells released directly by the parent plant, with no pollination needed at all.
Today we go further: some plants don't even need spores. A whole new plant can grow directly from a PIECE of the parent plant — no flower, no seed, no spore case required.

Why learn this? 🤔

This is exactly what Chapter 4, Section C of your school book covers next — and it explains everyday sights: why a piece of potato with "eyes" can grow into a whole new plant, and why gardeners often grow new plants from cuttings instead of waiting for seeds.
Real life: farmers and gardeners often WANT an exact copy of a great plant — a strawberry farmer doesn't want to gamble on seeds mixing genes unpredictably, so growing from runners and cuttings guarantees the new plant is identical to a proven, tasty parent.

Recap: Underground Stems Can Grow New Plants 🧅

From Unit 33: a potato TUBER is covered in "eyes" (buds) that can each sprout a whole new plant. An onion BULB, a ginger RHIZOME, and a corm can all do the same thing — because every piece with a bud can grow into a brand-new plant, without needing any flower, seed, or spore at all.
NEW idea: underground stems are just ONE example of "reproduction from other plant parts." There are more ways a new plant can grow directly from a piece of its parent — let's look at two more.

Runners & Cuttings 🍓✂️

A RUNNER is a long stem that grows sideways along the ground and sprouts roots (and a new plant) where it touches soil. A CUTTING is a piece of stem or leaf cut off on purpose and grown into a new plant.
Left: a runner stem reaches sideways from the parent plant and sprouts a new plant with its own roots where it touches soil. Right: a stem cutting placed in water grows new roots, and can then be planted.
Examples: a strawberry plant sends out runners that root into new strawberry plants nearby. A gardener can snip a healthy stem from a basil plant, stand it in a glass of water until roots grow, then plant it — a basil cutting.
Key idea: runners, cuttings, and underground stems (bulbs/tubers/rhizomes/corms) are all examples of REPRODUCTION FROM OTHER PLANT PARTS — no flower, seed, or spore is involved in any of them.

Comparing All the Ways Plants Reproduce ⚖️

MethodParents neededOffspring genetically identical to parent?
Seeds (pollination + fertilisation)TWO (pollen + ovule)No — genes mix, so offspring differ from both parents
Spores (ferns, mosses)ONEYes — no genes mixed in from a second parent
Other plant parts (bulbs, tubers, rhizomes, runners, cuttings)ONEYes — an exact genetic copy (a clone) of the parent
Key idea: reproduction from seeds is called SEXUAL reproduction (two parents' genes combine). Reproduction from spores or other plant parts is called ASEXUAL reproduction (only one parent is needed, and the new plant is genetically identical, or almost identical, to it).

Trap-Recognition Drill #10 🚨

Watch for words like ALWAYS, NEVER, ONLY, and ALL. A claim using one of these words is often too extreme to be true — even if it SOUNDS confident.

Drill. A student claims: "ALL new plants come from seeds." Is this claim true?

✔ Correct — this is a false absolute claim (the word "ALL" is the giveaway). Ferns, mosses, and plants grown from cuttings or bulbs all prove new plants don't always need a seed.
✘ Re-think it: does a potato with "eyes" or a fern's spore need a seed to grow into a new plant?

Which Method Is It? 🔍

How did the new plant actually form?
↓
From a seed, after pollination
SEXUAL — reproduction from seeds
From a tiny spore, no pollination
ASEXUAL — reproduction from spores
From a bulb, tuber, rhizome, runner, or cutting
ASEXUAL — reproduction from other plant parts
Key idea: only ONE of these three methods (seeds) is sexual reproduction. The other two are both asexual — but they're still two DIFFERENT methods from each other (spores vs. plant parts), so don't mix them up.
