Recap: Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants 🌸

From Unit 25: a flower has male parts (the STAMEN — anther makes pollen, sitting on a filament) and female parts (the PISTIL — a sticky stigma leading down a style into the ovary, which holds the ovule). POLLINATION is pollen moving from an anther to a stigma. FERTILISATION happens afterward, when the pollen travels down the style and joins with the ovule inside the ovary — that fertilised ovule becomes a new SEED.
NEW idea: this whole process needs a flower, pollen, AND pollination between two parts. But not every plant reproduces this way — some plants skip flowers and seeds completely!

Reproduction from Spores 🌿

Ferns and mosses don't have flowers at all. Instead, they make tiny, lightweight SPORES — and each spore can grow directly into a brand-new plant, with no pollination needed.
Spore cases sit in rows on the underside of a fern leaf. When ripe, they release tiny spores — and each spore that lands somewhere damp can grow directly into a brand-new fern plant, with no flower, pollen, or pollination involved at all.
SPORE: a tiny, lightweight reproductive cell made directly by the parent plant. Unlike a seed, a spore does NOT form after pollination — it's simply released and can grow into a new plant on its own, usually somewhere damp.
Key idea: mosses reproduce from spores too, released from tiny capsules on thin stalks. This is why moss and ferns often grow in damp, shady places — their spores need moisture to develop into new plants.
