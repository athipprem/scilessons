S.T.O.P.: How Scientists Read a Question

Last test you finished 20 questions in under 5 minutes. Four of your five misses were not about science at all. They were about reading. Here is the fix real scientists use.
S
Spot the twist. Find the one word that changes the answer.
Your real question (Unit 51, Q10): “Very salty water is dripped beside some cheek cells.” You chose the answer for pure water. The twist was “salty”.
T
Think first. Answer in your head before you read the options.
Unit 51, Q20: you chose 32,000 mm. That is 32 metres, a cell bigger than a classroom. Thinking “cells are tiny” first would have ruled it out.
O
Open all four. Read every option, even after one looks right.
Unit 51, Q15: the option you chose had the two labels swapped. Reading all four shows the one with the labels the right way round.
P
Prove it. Point to the word in the question that makes your answer right.
Unit 51, Q5: the question was about food, so the right option had to be about food.
Scientists check twice. It is how they get the right answer.
