Two Cells, Side by Side

Your body is built from animal cells. Plant cells and animal cells face the same danger, water, but they handle it in different ways. Knowing how explains why you get thirsty and why salt matters.
A leaf cell (left) and a cheek cell (right). Press the button.
No cell wall (a leaf cell's wall is made of cellulose). The soft outline is only the cell membrane.
No chloroplasts. It gets food by eating, not from light.
No big vacuole. Just cytoplasm around the nucleus.

Osmosis happens to both cells. The leaf cell never bursts, because its cell wall, built of cellulose, pushes back. The cheek cell has no wall to push back.

The Animal's Answer: Regulate (Route A)

A plant builds a wall. An animal cell has no wall, so the whole body must regulate the liquid around its cells. To regulate is to keep something steady: not too much, not too little. The kidneys do this job. They take extra water and extra salt out of the blood and send them away as urine. Then the blood carries liquid of just the right strength to every cell.
Pick one and watch the needle.
Too watery! The liquid around your cells is weaker than it should be. The kidneys make extra urine and send the extra water away. Back to just right. Your cells stay safe. Too salty! The liquid around your cells is stronger than it should be. You feel thirsty, so you drink water. Back to just right. Your cells stay safe.

Same Idea, as Steps (Route B)

Same idea, now as steps you can write down.
  1. Does the cell have a wall? Yes: osmosis can pull water in, but the wall stops the cell bursting.
  2. No wall (an animal cell)? Big changes in the water around it would make osmosis burst or shrivel it.
  3. So the animal's body must regulate that water: keep it the same strength all the time.
  4. In your body, the kidneys do that job.

Why No Chloroplasts? Why No Wall?

No chloroplasts. A chloroplast holds green chlorophyll and uses light to make food (photosynthesis, Unit 8). An animal gets its food by eating, and most of its cells sit in the dark inside the body. A chloroplast there would have no light to use.
No cell wall. Animals move. Their cells must bend and change shape, and a stiff wall would stop that. That is why an animal needs the water answer you just met: its body must regulate the water, not build a wall.
