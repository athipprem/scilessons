Quick Recall from Lesson 1 & Unit 30 🔁

🔁 Remember Lesson 1? Being attracted to a magnet doesn't make an object a magnet — only something that can pull OTHER magnetic objects on its own is a true magnet. 🔁 Remember the simple 2-magnet check? Two bar magnets touch. If the two facing ends are the SAME (N–N or S–S), they repel. If they're DIFFERENT (N–S), they attract. You check this ONE join at a time.

Today's chains are trickier: THREE magnets touching in a row means TWO joins to check, not just one — and sometimes one join attracts while the other repels, in the very same chain. The method is exactly the same, just repeated twice, very carefully.

Why learn this? 🤔

ASMOPSS papers love multi-magnet chain diagrams because they test whether you can apply the SAME simple rule (same poles repel, different poles attract) carefully and repeatedly, rather than just once. Rushing through a chain of 3+ magnets is one of the most common ways marks are lost — even when the underlying rule is fully understood.
Real life: engineers stacking magnetic parts in a machine, or scientists designing a magnetic sorting line at a recycling plant, both need to predict exactly how a whole ROW of magnets or magnetic objects will behave — not just a single pair.

Reading a Harder 3-Magnet Chain, Step by Step 🧲🧲🧲

GOLDEN RULE for any chain: check ONE join at a time. Every single magnet always has a North end AND a South end — never just one.
Three bar magnets P, Q, and R touch end to end in a straight line. P's far-left end is labelled N. We're TOLD that the P–Q join attracts, and the Q–R join repels. Every other end is a mystery (?) — can you work them all out?
Step 1: P's left end is N (given). Every magnet has TWO opposite poles, so P's right end (touching Q) must be S.
Step 2: The P–Q join ATTRACTS, which only happens when the two touching ends are DIFFERENT. P's right end is S, so Q's left end (touching P) must be N.
Step 3: Q also has two opposite poles — its left is N, so Q's right end (touching R) must be S.
Step 4: The Q–R join REPELS, which only happens when the two touching ends are the SAME. Q's right end is S, so R's left end (touching Q) must ALSO be S.
Final answer: P = N–S, Q = N–S, R = S–(unknown far end). Notice Join 1 and Join 2 used OPPOSITE logic (different poles attract vs same poles repel) — that's exactly why rushing through a 3-magnet chain trips people up. Slow down and re-apply the rule fresh at EVERY join.

Quick Check: using the SAME chain above, what is Magnet Q's LEFT end (the one touching P)?

✔ Correct — Join 1 attracts, and P's right end is S, so Q's touching left end must be the DIFFERENT pole: North.
✘ Look at Step 2 again — the P–Q join attracts, meaning the two touching ends are different poles. Since P's end is S, Q's end must be N.

Forces Recap: Contact, Non-Contact & Balanced 👋

A FORCE is a push or a pull. CONTACT forces need touching; NON-CONTACT forces work from a distance. BALANCED forces don't change motion; UNBALANCED forces do.
🤝
CONTACT forces
friction, a push, a pull, air resistance — objects must be touching
🌌
NON-CONTACT forces
gravity, magnetism, static electric attraction — work even at a distance
Balanced vs unbalanced: a book resting still on a table has gravity pulling it down and the table pushing it up — BALANCED, so it doesn't move. Push that same book sideways and nothing pushes back equally — UNBALANCED, so it starts sliding.

Combining Forces Recap ➕➖

Forces in the SAME direction ADD UP fully. Forces in OPPOSITE directions SUBTRACT (the smaller cancels out part of the bigger).
Worked example (from Unit 29): in a tug-of-war, if Team A pulls with 10N and Team B pulls with 6N in the opposite direction, the rope moves toward Team A with a leftover force of 10N − 6N = 4N.
THREE forces? Sort into direction-groups FIRST. Add up everyone pulling one way, add up everyone pulling the other way, THEN subtract the smaller total from the bigger total. Never just add every number together regardless of direction.

Combining Forces: Decision Tree 🌳

Are all the forces pulling/pushing in the SAME direction?
YES — every force points the same way
ADD them all together
NO — some forces point opposite ways
Sort into 2 direction-groups, add each group, then SUBTRACT smaller from bigger
How to use this: always sort every force into its direction FIRST, before doing any arithmetic — this is the exact step that trips people up when three or more forces are involved at once.

1. Three bar magnets A, B, and C touch end to end. A's left end is S. The A–B join repels. The B–C join attracts. What is B's LEFT end (touching A)?

✔ Correct — A's left is S, so A's right (touching B) must be N. The A–B join REPELS, which means the two touching ends are the SAME pole, so B's left end must also be N.
✘ Careful — A's left is S, so A's right (touching B) must be N (a magnet's two ends are always opposite). The A–B join REPELS, meaning the two touching ends are the SAME pole — so B's left end must also be North, matching A's right end.

2. Which of these is a NON-CONTACT force?

✔ Correct — magnetism works even when objects aren't touching, making it a non-contact force.
✘ Re-check Screen 5 — friction, pushing, and air resistance all need contact; only magnetism (and gravity, and static attraction) can act from a distance.

3. Two teams pull a rope in opposite directions: Team Red pulls with 15N, Team Blue pulls with 9N. What happens to the rope?

✔ Correct — opposite forces subtract: 15N − 9N = 6N leftover, moving toward the stronger side, Team Red.
✘ Re-check Screen 6 — opposite-direction forces SUBTRACT, and the rope moves toward whichever side pulled harder.

4. Three forces act on a toy car: 8N pushing right, 3N pushing right, and 5N pushing left. What is the correct FIRST step to combine them?

✔ Correct — always sort forces into direction-groups FIRST, before doing any adding or subtracting.
✘ Re-check Screen 7 — with 3+ forces, the first step is always sorting by direction, never just adding every number blindly.

5. A chain of two touching bar magnets has their facing ends both labelled S. What will happen to this chain?

✔ Correct — matching poles (S–S) always repel, so this chain would push apart at that join.
✘ Re-check Screen 4 — SAME poles facing each other (like S–S) always repel, never attract.
