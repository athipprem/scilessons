Why Scatter Seeds?

A farmer who sows seeds too close together loses plants. Plants have their own way to avoid this: seed dispersal.

Which plants look healthier?

Plants in Pot A do not need to compete for water, light and nutrients. Pot B has overcrowding: too many seedlings in one small place. A seedling is a young plant that has just grown from a seed.

If all the seeds dropped under the parent, there would be overcrowding. The seedlings would compete for water, light and nutrients, and grow unhealthy. So plants practise seed dispersal: they spread their seeds away from the parent. They do it by wind, by water, by animals or by splitting.

Four Ways to Travel

By windangsana, shorea, lalang
By watercoconut, pong pong, nipah, lotus
By animalsrambutan, guava, durian, mimosa, love grass
By splittingflame of the forest, rubber, African tulip, kapok

Seed dispersal has four ways. Wind: a winged fruit glides, and small light seeds float away. Water: the coconut has a light, fibrous husk, so it floats across the sea. Animals: animals eat juicy fruits and throw the seeds away or pass them out in droppings. Some fruits have hooks that cling to the fur of animals, or to clothes. Splitting: some pods split with explosive force. Others split without force, and then the wind carries the seeds.

What Does a Seed Need? Route 1: Switches

A farmer who sows seeds in dry soil wastes them. A seed waits until the conditions are right, and then it germinates: the first root and the first shoot start to grow.
Flip the switches. Which ones make the seed germinate? Try the light too.
Germination needs water, oxygen and the right temperature. Light is not needed to start.

Route 2: The 3-Line Rule

A seed germinates when it has all three: 1. water 2. oxygen 3. the right temperature

Germination needs water, oxygen and the right temperature. Light is not needed to start: a seed under the soil has none. Check the three lines before you answer.

Inside the Seed

A seed is a baby plant with a packed lunch. The seed coat is the hard cover. Inside, the seed leaves store food. Seed leaves are the biggest part of a bean seed. The first shoot will grow up. The first root will grow down: the root grows downwards as soon as the seed germinates. The seed began as an ovule: the ovary has one or more ovules.

Until it can make its own food, the seedling gets food from the seed leaves.
