Where Do Seeds Come From?

Every bean, pumpkin and apple began as a flower. A farmer gets fruit only if the flowers do their job, so we start inside the flower.

Young plants grow from seeds. Seeds are made by the reproductive parts of a flower. In some plants the male and female parts are on a single flower: hibiscus and morning glory. In others, the male and female parts are on separate flowers: maize and cucumber.

The Same Flower, as a List

Female part
  • The stigma is at the top.
  • The ovary has one or more ovules.
  • Each ovule contains an egg. The egg is the female reproductive cell.
Male part
  • A thin stalk, with an anther at the tip.
  • The anther contains pollen sacs.
  • The sacs hold pollen, the male reproductive cells.
Careful: the anther is NOT part of the female part.
The ovary has one or more ovules, each ovule contains an egg, and the anther contains pollen sacs.

Pollination

A flower cannot walk to find a mate. Pollen is small, light and powdery, so something must carry it. Wind or animals such as insects carry pollen to the stigma. The transfer of pollen from the anther to the stigma is called pollination.

Pollination by insects
Pollen sits on the anthers of a hibiscus.
A butterfly lands to drink. Pollen rubs onto its body.
It flies to another hibiscus, carrying pollen.
Pollen lands on the stigma. This is pollination: the transfer of pollen from the anther to the stigma.
Pollination by wind
Pollen is light and powdery.
The wind blows it off the anthers.
It drifts through the air.
Some lands on a stigma. Pollination again.
A butterfly passes through complete: egg, larva, pupa, adult. As an adult it drinks nectar and carries pollen.
When pollen goes to a different flower, that is cross-pollination. Lesson 2 explains cross-pollination.
Book box: cedar trees release great amounts of very light pollen into the air every year. Wind carries it, and it makes many people sneeze.

Fertilisation, Fruit and Seeds

After pollination, the pollen grain enters an ovule. The nucleus of the pollen grain fuses with the nucleus of the egg. The result is a fertilised egg. This is fertilisation.

Old mistake from Chapter 3: two eggs never join. Here the male cell is the pollen grain, and the female cell is the egg. Like every cell, each has a nucleus, and the cell membrane controls what goes in and out.
A pollen grain has landed on the stigma.
The pollen grain enters the ovule.
The nucleus of the pollen grain fuses with the nucleus of the egg: fertilisation.
The ovary develops into a fruit, and the ovules develop into seeds.

After fertilisation, the ovary develops into a fruit, and the ovules develop into seeds. Each seed carries a fertilised egg that can grow into a new plant. The fruit protects the seeds until they are released when the fruit is ripe.

Plants and Animals Compared

In animals, males make sperm and females make eggs. In flowering plants, the male parts make pollen and the female parts make eggs. Pollen has a similar job to sperm. In both, a new living thing forms when the male and female cells fuse.
