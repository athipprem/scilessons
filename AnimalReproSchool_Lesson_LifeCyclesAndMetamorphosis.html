Quick Recap 🔁

From Lesson 1: animals reproduce SEXUALLY — a male parent's sperm joins with a female parent's egg in FERTILISATION, which can happen INTERNALLY (inside the body — mammals, birds, reptiles) or EXTERNALLY (outside the body, usually in water — most fish, most amphibians).
Today we go further: once an egg is fertilised, what happens as the young animal actually GROWS UP? Does it always look like a tiny copy of its parents — or can it look completely different along the way?

Why learn this? 🤔

Life cycles are exactly what your school's Chapter 3 covers next, and they explain everyday sights: why a caterpillar looks nothing like a butterfly, why a chick already looks like a tiny chicken, and why a tadpole swims but a frog hops.
Real life: gardeners protect caterpillars because they know a butterfly's whole life cycle depends on that in-between stage — and farmers time egg-collecting around exactly which life cycle their animals follow.

Type 1: No Metamorphosis 🐕

Some animals hatch or are born already looking like a SMALLER VERSION of their parents. As they grow, they just get BIGGER — their body shape doesn't change.
No metamorphosis: the young animal keeps the SAME basic body shape all the way to adulthood — it just grows bigger.
Examples: humans, cats, dogs, chickens, and guppy fish. A kitten already looks like a small cat; a chick already looks like a small chicken.

Type 2: Incomplete Metamorphosis 🦗

Some animals hatch from an egg into a NYMPH — which already looks similar to the adult, just smaller and without full wings — then grows in stages into an adult. THREE stages: egg → nymph → adult.
Incomplete metamorphosis: the nymph already looks like a smaller version of the adult — same basic body plan — it just grows bigger and grows wings, over several stages.
Examples: cockroaches, grasshoppers, and dragonflies all go through incomplete metamorphosis.

Type 3: Complete Metamorphosis 🦋

Some animals hatch into a LARVA that looks COMPLETELY DIFFERENT from the adult, then pass through a resting PUPA stage before finally becoming the adult. FOUR stages: egg → larva → pupa → adult.
Complete metamorphosis: the larva looks nothing like the adult. The pupa is a resting stage where the body completely rebuilds itself before the adult finally emerges.
Examples: a butterfly (egg → caterpillar [larva] → chrysalis [pupa] → butterfly) and a housefly (egg → maggot [larva] → pupa → fly).
A frog is a special example too: egg → tadpole → froglet → adult frog. The tadpole looks nothing like the adult frog (no legs, has a tail, breathes underwater) — a dramatic change, just like an insect's complete metamorphosis, even though a frog uses slightly different stage names.

Trap-Recognition Drill #9 🚨

Watch for words like ALWAYS, NEVER, ONLY, and ALL. A claim using one of these words is often too extreme to be true — even if it SOUNDS confident.

Drill. A student claims: "ALL animals go through metamorphosis as they grow up." Is this claim true?

✔ Correct — this is a false absolute claim (the word "ALL" is the giveaway). Plenty of animals, like humans, cats, and chickens, follow the "no metamorphosis" life cycle and never change body shape as they grow.
✘ Re-think it: does a kitten look completely different from an adult cat, the way a caterpillar looks different from a butterfly?
