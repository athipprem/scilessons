Heredity: Why You Look Like Your Family

Why learn this? It explains why you have Dad’s ears or Mum’s smile, and why you are still not a copy of either.
Heredity is the passing of traits from parents to young. A trait is a feature, such as eye colour.

Young are like their parents, but not exactly the same. Eye colour, hair colour, hair type, ear shape and height (partly) are inherited traits you can see. Inherited traits are passed on in the genes. Traits you pick up in life are acquired.

Deal a Zibbit Baby

A Zibbit has two cards for each feature. Press the button.
The cards are genes. Inherited traits are passed on in the genes, on chromosomes inside each cell.

The Card Rules

Think of a recipe card. Each feature has two cards, and you get one from each parent. In Lesson 2, some cards are dominant (strong) and some recessive (hidden).
  1. Each feature is a pair of cards (genes).
  2. Mum gives 1 card and Dad gives 1 card: half from mum, half from dad.
  3. Different picks make different babies: a different mix for each child.

Which kind of twins look alike?
Identical twins start as one egg, split in two, so they have the same cards and the same inherited traits.

Inherited or Acquired?

The test: could it get into the egg or sperm? Tap a card, then its bin.
Acquired traits are things you learn or pick up. They can't get into the egg or sperm. Inherited traits are already in the genes.

Genes Are Not the Whole Story

Two cuttings from one plant. One is watered and one is left thirsty. They grow differently. Genes and surroundings both matter: inherited traits come from the genes, acquired ones from life.
