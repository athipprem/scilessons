The Cell's Loop

Every part of you grew from cells that copy themselves. That is how you grow taller and how a scrape heals. Press Run and watch one cell live one full loop.
The whole ring is one cell cycle. The long green part is the cell growing and doing its job. The short red slice is dividing.

Run It Again, With Captions

Watch what happens to the chromosomes.

The Loop Card

Same story, one step per line.
  1. Grow and do its job. That is most of its life.
  2. Copy every chromosome inside the nucleus.
  3. Split into two identical cells.
  4. Each new cell starts the same loop again. That is why it is called the cell cycle.

Photo: Catch Cells at Random

Press Photo three times. Count the red-outlined cells each time.
Only a few cells are dividing in each photo, because most of its life, a cell is growing and doing its job. The red slice is 1 in 10 of the ring, so a photo catches about 1 in 10 cells dividing: 2 of 20.

Yeast: Budding

Yeast is a fungus made of one cell. It makes new cells by budding. Press Run.
In budding, a bump grows out of the side and the bud pinches off. The bud is an identical copy of its one parent, just like runners in Unit 46.

Plants: The Growing Points

Watch the little clocks. Fast spinning means fast dividing.
The growing points are the root and shoot tips. That is where the plant gets longer.
