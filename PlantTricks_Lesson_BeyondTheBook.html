The Danger of Copies

A banana farm may grow thousands of plants from suckers. Every sucker comes from one parent, so the young are identical. Is that safe? Send one disease to two fields.

A disease can wipe out a whole field of identical copies. Seeds come from two parents, so the young are a little different, and some can resist. This links to Chapter 3: members of the same kind are not all the same.

Where Spores Grow

A spore is tiny and light, and it lands anywhere. Does every spore grow? Let spores land on two walls.
Damp, shady wall
Dry, sunny wall
Press the button.
Spores need water to start growing. A moss spore on a damp, shady wall can grow into new moss. One on a dry, sunny wall cannot. Spore bags on the underside of a frond release them. Remember: some non-flowering plants such as ferns and moss reproduce from spores, and the spores need damp places.

Real Size

A microscope photo makes a spore look big. The magnification tells you how many times bigger the photo is than the real thing. To find the real size, work backwards: divide by the magnification.

Real size of a spore

Watch me
Step 1. A moss spore photo is taken at ×300. It shows the spore 6 mm wide. The real spore is much smaller, so the answer is smaller than 6.
Step 2. The photo is 300 times bigger than real. To go back, divide by the magnification: 6 ÷ 300.
Step 3. 6 ÷ 300 = 0.02. The real spore is 0.02 mm wide.
Step 4. Size check: 0.02 × 300 = 6, which matches the photo. If you multiplied instead, you would get 1,800 mm, far bigger than the photo. That cannot be right.

Trap Drill #25 and Self-Check

Check every statement. Copy the question's words. Say how. Ask: and why is that good?
Trap-Recognition Drill #25: big words. ALWAYS, NEVER, ONLY and ALL are big words, and one exception breaks them. Example: “Every plant ALWAYS needs a flower to make a new plant.” A bryophyllum leaf makes a new plant with no flower, so that is false.
