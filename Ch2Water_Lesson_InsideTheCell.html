What Fills the Vacuole 💧

The vacuole is a sac. Watch what happens when it fills.
Cell sap is the watery liquid that fills the vacuole. As it fills, it pushes outward against the wall — that outward push is what keeps the whole cell firm.

Wall vs. Membrane, as a Picture 🧱

Same two particles — one small (water), one big (dissolved sugar) — hitting each layer.
Key idea: the wall's gaps are big enough for everything — that's fully permeable. The membrane's gaps are small enough to block the big sugar particle but let the small water particle through — that's partially permeable. Because the wall is fully permeable, it never does any sorting itself; the real gatekeeper is the partially permeable membrane sitting just inside it.

Turgid ↔ Flaccid, on a Slider 🎛️

Drag the slider. Watch the vacuole, not just the words.
WILTING (in between)
Key idea: turgid and flaccid aren't two switches — they're two ends of the same slider. Every amount of water in between shows up as a slightly bigger or smaller vacuole. That water only moves across because the membrane is partially permeable — it lets water through but keeps the bigger dissolved particles out.

Trap-Recognition Drill #13 🚨

Watch for ALWAYS, NEVER, ONLY, EVERY. A claim using one of these words is often too extreme.

Drill. A student claims: "The cell membrane and the cell wall are ALWAYS made of the exact same material." True or false?

✔ Correct — this is a false absolute claim. The wall and membrane are two separate layers doing two separate jobs.
✘ Re-think it: does the wall block anything the way the membrane does?
