The Survival Pond

Every living thing dies one day. Reproduction makes a new living thing, its offspring, so its species carries on. A species is one kind of living thing.
A pond fish lays 1,000 eggs. Each dot is 10 eggs. Press Next danger four times.

Few Young, Lots of Care

Now an elephant mother. She has only 4 calves in her whole life. Press both dangers.
few young, lots of care
2 parents need 2 grown-up young. 3 offspring is enough, so the species is not extinct and this keeps the species going.
Two plans, one goal: many young, little care or few young, lots of care. Both end with enough offspring grown up to replace the parents. The care a parent gives is called parental care.

The Survival Card

Same pond, now as steps you can do on paper.
  1. Count the eggs.
  2. Find the share that grows up. “1 in N” means fewer, so divide: survivors = eggs ÷ N.
  3. Compare with the parents. At least 2 grown-up offspring: this keeps the species going. Fewer than 2: the numbers shrink, and the species may become extinct.

How many young grow up? Step by step

“1 in 100 grow up” means out of every 100 eggs, just 1 becomes an adult.

👀 Watch me

A pond fish lays 1,000 eggs. 1 in 100 grow up. How many survivors?
1More or fewer than 1,000? Fewer: most never grow up.
2Fewer means divide by 100.
3Write it: survivors = eggs ÷ 100, so 1,000 ÷ 100 = 10.
4Size check: 10 × 100 = 1,000. That is back to the eggs.
5Compare: 10 is more than 2, so this keeps the species going.
