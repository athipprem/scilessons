Quick Recall from Unit 1 🔁

🔁 Remember Unit 1? A potato is NOT a root — it's a STEM that grows underground and stores food. The test that proves it: a stem has BUDS (the "eyes" on a potato, where new shoots can sprout). Roots never have buds. Onion, ginger, and turmeric are underground stems too — while carrot, radish, and sweet potato are true roots.

Today we go deeper: underground stems actually come in FOUR different shapes, and there's an easy way to tell true roots and true stems apart even when they look confusingly similar (like potato vs sweet potato).

Why learn this? 🤔

Farmers, chefs, and gardeners all rely on knowing exactly which part of a vegetable they're growing or cooking — a bulb like an onion is planted differently from a tuber like a potato, and a rhizome like ginger can be replanted from a single cut piece because every piece with a bud can grow a whole new plant. ASMOPSS often tests this exact kind of "classify the vegetable" applied reasoning, not just memorised names.
Real life: when you plant a piece of ginger with a bud on it, a whole new ginger plant grows from that one bud — that's only possible because ginger is a STEM (stems have buds), and it's exactly the same reason potato "eyes" can grow new potato plants.

Four Types of Underground Stem 🥔

All four are STEMS (they all have buds!) — but each one is shaped completely differently.
All four have buds somewhere on them — that's the proof they're stems, not roots.

🥔 TUBER

A swollen underground stem, round and lumpy, with buds ("eyes") scattered all over its surface.
Example: potato

🧅 BULB

Made of layers wrapped tightly around a very short stem at the base — the layers are actually modified leaves, not the stem itself!
Example: onion, garlic

🫚 RHIZOME

A stem that grows sideways, just under the soil, with buds spaced out along its length that can each sprout a new shoot.
Example: ginger, turmeric

🌿 CORM

A solid, swollen stem base (not made of layers like a bulb) with a few buds on top and sides.
Example: taro, crocus
Easy way to remember bulb vs the rest: cut a bulb in half and you'll see rings, like an onion — those rings are layers of modified leaves protecting the tiny short stem at the very center. Cut a tuber, rhizome, or corm in half and it's solid all the way through, because the WHOLE thing is stem tissue.

Storage Roots vs. Stem Tubers 🥕

Potato and sweet potato look similar and are both called "potato" — but one is a STEM and the other is a TRUE ROOT.

🥕 Storage Root

Examples: sweet potato, carrot, radish

These are TRUE ROOTS that have swollen up to store extra food for the plant. Apply the buds test: a storage root has NO buds anywhere on its surface — smooth all over, sometimes with thin little root hairs, but never an "eye."

🥔 Stem Tuber

Example: potato

This is a swollen underground STEM. Apply the buds test: a stem tuber is covered in "eyes" (buds) — each one is a real bud that can sprout a brand new plant, exactly like a bud on a branch above ground.

The one test that always works: does it have buds ("eyes")? Buds → stem (tuber, bulb, rhizome, or corm). No buds anywhere → true root (a storage root). This is the exact same buds/eyes rule from Unit 1 — it still works perfectly here, even on tricky look-alikes.

1. A vegetable is smooth all over with no buds anywhere on its surface. What is it most likely?

✔ Correct — no buds anywhere means it's a true root that stores food, like a sweet potato or carrot.

2. You cut a vegetable in half and see rings, like layers wrapped around a tiny short stem in the center. What type of underground stem is this?

✔ Correct — layered rings around a short central stem is exactly how a bulb, like an onion, is built.
✘ Re-check Screen 4 — rings of modified leaves around a tiny stem is the bulb's signature.

3. Why is a potato classified as a stem and not a root, even though it grows underground?

✔ Correct — buds are the proof: only stems have buds, and a potato's "eyes" are real buds.
✘ Re-check Screen 2/5 — the buds test is what separates stems from roots, not colour, depth, or shape.

4. Ginger grows sideways just under the soil, with buds spaced out along its length. What type of underground stem is ginger?

✔ Correct — growing sideways with buds spaced along its length is the rhizome pattern, just like ginger and turmeric.
✘ Re-check Screen 4 — a sideways-growing stem with spaced-out buds is a rhizome.

5. A gardener cuts a piece of ginger that includes one bud, and plants it. A whole new ginger plant grows from that piece. What does this prove about ginger?

✔ Correct — a new plant sprouting from a bud is only possible because ginger is a stem, and stems are the only part with buds.
✘ Re-check Screen 3/5 — the buds test again: sprouting from a bud proves it's a stem, not a root.
