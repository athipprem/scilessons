What Is a Mixture? 🥣

Remember from Unit 9: a PHYSICAL change doesn't make a new substance — it just changes how something looks or is arranged. A CHEMICAL change makes something genuinely new that can't easily be undone.
A MIXTURE is two or more substances physically combined together — NOT chemically joined. Each substance keeps its own properties, and mixing is a physical change.
Even after shaking the jar, you could still pick out every individual nut and raisin by hand — nothing new was formed, and nothing changed what a nut or a raisin actually is.

More examples of mixtures: salt and pepper shaken together, sand and pebbles, a bowl of mixed vegetables, the air you breathe (a mixture of gases).

Key idea: in every one of these, you could — at least in principle — sort the ingredients back apart, because nothing chemically changed. That's the test for "is this a mixture?"

What Is a Solution? 🥤

Remember from Unit 2: when sugar dissolves in water, it doesn't disappear or get destroyed — it spreads out into tiny particles too small to see. A covered cup weighs exactly the same before and after dissolving.
A SOLUTION is a special, EVEN kind of mixture where a SOLUTE (the thing that dissolves) spreads out completely and invisibly into a SOLVENT (the thing it dissolves into) — and it won't settle back out on its own.
Sugar is SOLUBLE in water — it dissolves and vanishes from sight, forming a true solution. Sand is INSOLUBLE in water — it never dissolves, no matter how long you stir, and eventually settles back to the bottom.

Vocabulary check: SOLUTE = the thing that dissolves (sugar). SOLVENT = the thing it dissolves into (water). SOLUBLE = able to dissolve in a given solvent. INSOLUBLE = unable to dissolve in it.

Mixture or Solution? 🌳

Does it dissolve — spreading out so evenly you can't see separate pieces, and staying that way without settling back out?
↓
YES
It's a SOLUTION — a solute fully dissolved in a solvent (e.g. salt water, sugar water)
NO
It's just a MIXTURE, not a solution — the pieces stay visible/separate, or they eventually settle back out (e.g. sand and water, salt and pepper)
Key idea: every solution IS a mixture, but not every mixture is a solution — a solution is the special case where the solute truly dissolves.

Worked Example: Nan's Three Cups 🥄

Scenario: Nan sets up three cups: Cup 1 — sugar stirred into warm water. Cup 2 — sand stirred into water. Cup 3 — salt and pepper shaken together in a dry jar (no liquid at all).

Step 1 — are all three mixtures? Yes — every one combines two or more substances physically, with no new substance formed.

Step 2 — which one is a SOLUTION? Only Cup 1. The sugar dissolves completely, spreads out evenly, and disappears from sight — it won't settle back out.

Step 3 — why isn't Cup 2 a solution? Sand is insoluble in water. It never dissolves — it just sits mixed in, and if left alone it settles to the bottom.

Step 4 — why isn't Cup 3 a solution? There's no dissolving happening at all — salt and pepper are two dry solids shaken together. You can still see, and even pick out, individual grains and flakes.
