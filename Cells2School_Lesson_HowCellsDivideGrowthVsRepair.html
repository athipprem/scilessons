Quick Recap 🔁

From Lesson 1: CELL DIVISION is when one cell splits into two identical new cells. Living things use it for two reasons: GROWTH (getting bigger, mainly by making MORE cells) and REPAIR (replacing damaged or lost cells).
Today we go further: what actually HAPPENS, step by step, inside a cell as it divides — and does this process ever fully stop?

Why learn this? 🤔

Understanding HOW cell division actually works — not just that it happens — is exactly what your school's Chapter 2 covers next, and it explains everyday things like why a cut takes a few days to heal instead of happening instantly.
Real life: a skinned knee doesn't heal in one second — it takes DAYS, because cell division is a step-by-step process that takes real time to make enough new cells to close the wound.

How Cell Division Works ⚙️

Cell division happens in three simple stages: the cell GROWS, then it COPIES its parts, then it SPLITS into two.
Stage 1: the cell grows a little bigger. Stage 2: the cell makes a copy of everything inside it. Stage 3: the cell splits into two separate, identical new cells.
Key idea: this whole three-stage process takes real TIME — it isn't instant. That's exactly why healing an injury takes days rather than happening in a flash.

Growth Over a Lifetime 📈

Cell division happens FASTEST during childhood (when the body is growing quickly) but it NEVER completely stops, even in adults.
Childhood: cell division happens very fast — this is why babies and children grow noticeably taller and bigger, year after year.
Adulthood: a fully-grown adult stops getting taller, because growth-related division has slowed right down. But cell division does NOT stop completely — it continues for REPAIR: healing wounds, replacing worn-out skin cells, and replacing old blood cells with new ones.
Key idea: "growth has stopped" is not the same thing as "cell division has stopped." An adult isn't getting any taller, but their body is still dividing cells every single day just to replace and repair.

Trap-Recognition Drill #8 🚨

Watch for words like ALWAYS, NEVER, ONLY, and ALL. A claim using one of these words is often too extreme to be true — even if it SOUNDS confident.

Drill. A student claims: "Once someone becomes a fully-grown adult, their cells NEVER divide again for the rest of their life." Is this claim true?

✔ Correct — this is a false absolute claim (the word "NEVER" is the giveaway). Adult cells stop dividing for GROWTH but keep dividing for REPAIR throughout life.
✘ Re-think it: growth-division slows in adults, but does the body ever stop needing to heal cuts or replace old skin/blood cells?
