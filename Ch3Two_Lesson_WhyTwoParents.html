The Beetle Garden

Imagine a garden where every beetle has one parent. (Some insects, like aphids, really do this.) Next door, beetles have two parents. When the world changes, which garden does better?
Variation = small differences between living things of the same kind.
Why it matters: when the world changes, someone has to be different enough to survive.

The Two-Parent Card

Same gardens, as steps.
  1. Count the parents.
  2. One parent gives identical copies. Two parents give young that each get a different mix. That mix is variation.
  3. When the world changes, ask: is anyone different enough to survive?
How many parents?
↓
One: one parent, identical young
Two: two parents, different young
After a change, variation means some survive. Identical young all share the same weakness. Plants too: plantlets are copies, and seeds mix two parents.

Plants Do It Too

A spider plant makes young in two ways.
plantlets are copies
seeds mix two parents
Plantlets hang from the parent on long stems: plantlets are copies. Its flowers take in pollen from another spider plant and make seeds: seeds mix two parents.
Tap a card, then tap its bin.

How many young survive? Step by step

survivors = total ÷ N
“1 in 4 is brown” means out of every 4 young, 1 is brown.

👀 Watch me

40 young beetles. 1 in 4 is brown, so it hides from the birds. How many survive?
1More or fewer than 40? Fewer: only 1 in every 4.
2Fewer means divide by 4.
3Write it: survivors = total ÷ 4, so 40 ÷ 4 = 10.
4Size check: 10 × 4 = 40. Back to the total.
5Compare: 10 is more than 0. Variation means some survive.
