The Whole Cycle

Every fruit you eat began as a flower. Know the four steps in order and you can answer many questions in the Chapter 4 test. This lesson gives you the whole chapter in one go.
Pollination starts the cycle. Tap a word, then tap its oval.
↗↘ The cycle ↖↙
The order: pollination, fertilisation, seed dispersal and germination.
Remember. Pollination is the transfer of pollen from the anther to the stigma. In fertilisation, a male reproductive cell fuses with a female reproductive cell. Then the ovules develop into seeds and the ovary becomes the fruit.

Too Many Plants in One Spot

Seeds that all fall under the parent plant have a problem. Compare the two pots.
Pot 1: 4 plants
Pot 2: 15 plants

Why are the plants in Pot 1 healthier?

Too many plants growing close together compete for water, light and nutrients. So plants spread their seeds: by wind, by water, by animals or by splitting.

Route B: Three Questions

Same question, a different way. Ask where does the new plant start, then follow the three questions.
1. Does it have flowers? Yes: it starts from a seed.
2. No. Are there spore bags under its leaves? Yes: it starts from a spore.
3. No. Does a new shoot start from a bud, the base or a leaf? Yes: it starts from a plant part: a sucker, an underground stem or a leaf.

Real Size

A microscope photo makes a pollen grain look big. The magnification tells you how many times bigger the photo is. To find the real size, work backwards: divide by the magnification.

Real size of a pollen grain

Watch me
Step 1. A photo at ×100 shows a grain 5 mm wide. The real grain is much smaller, so the answer is smaller than 5.
Step 2. The photo is 100 times bigger than real. To go back, divide by the magnification: 5 ÷ 100.
Step 3. 5 ÷ 100 = 0.05. The real grain is 0.05 mm wide.
Step 4. Size check: 0.05 × 100 = 5, which matches the photo. Multiplying instead would give 500 mm, bigger than the photo. That cannot be right.
