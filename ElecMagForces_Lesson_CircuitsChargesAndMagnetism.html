Quick Recall from Units 6 & 23 🔁

🔁 Remember Unit 6? Electricity only flows around a COMPLETE, unbroken loop — one gap anywhere (an open switch, a missing wire, a snapped connection) and nothing works. Materials are either CONDUCTORS (let electricity flow) or INSULATORS (block it). 🔁 Remember Unit 23? Rubbing two objects together can move electrons from one to the other — whichever object GAINS electrons becomes NEGATIVE, whichever LOSES electrons becomes POSITIVE. Same charges REPEL, different charges ATTRACT. A charged object can even pull a NEUTRAL object closer without touching it — that's induction.

Today, we're going to bring those two topics back to full strength with fresh scenarios, then push magnetism a level deeper than Unit 3 did — into territory that ASMOPSS papers have specifically tested and that needs one more careful look.

Circuits Recap: Complete the Loop 🔋

A circuit needs a POWER SOURCE, a PATH (wires), and something to USE the energy — all connected in ONE unbroken loop.
Battery → wire → bulb → wire → back to the battery. Every part is connected with no gaps, so electricity flows all the way around and the bulb lights up.
Is the loop fully connected, with no gaps anywhere?
Yes — every connection is joined, switch closed
Bulb lights up ✓
No — a wire is missing, or a switch is open
Bulb stays off ✗

Conductors vs Insulators Recap 🔍

A CONDUCTOR lets electricity flow through it easily. An INSULATOR blocks electricity from flowing.
⚡
CONDUCTORS
most metals: copper wire, steel, iron, aluminium foil
🚫
INSULATORS
plastic, rubber, wood, glass, dry paper
Careful: a wire's plastic COATING is an insulator on purpose — it stops electricity from escaping and keeps you safe from shocks, while the metal wire INSIDE it is the conductor doing the actual work.

Static Charges Recap ⚡

Rubbing transfers ELECTRONS. Same charges REPEL. Different charges ATTRACT. A charge can even pull a NEUTRAL object without touching it — induction.

Fresh example: a girl rubs a balloon on her wool jumper. The balloon gains extra electrons and becomes NEGATIVELY charged. She holds the balloon near a neutral wall.

The balloon's negative charge pushes the wall's own electrons slightly further away, leaving the near side of the wall slightly positive — and since opposite charges attract, the balloon sticks. This is INDUCTION: the balloon never touched the wall's charges directly, it just pushed them around.

Key rule to keep straight: two objects that are BOTH negatively charged (or both positively charged) will always repel — even from a distance. A charged object and a NEUTRAL object will always attract, thanks to induction.

Magnet or Just Magnetic? A Tricky New Idea 🧲

BEING ATTRACTED TO A MAGNET does NOT make an object a magnet itself. Only some magnetic materials are ACTUALLY magnets — the rest just get pulled toward one.

Remember from Unit 3: iron, steel, nickel, and cobalt are "magnetic materials" — a magnet pulls on them. But here's the part Unit 3 didn't cover: being pulled BY a magnet is completely different from BEING a magnet.

The key test: a real magnet can pull on OTHER magnetic objects all by itself, from a distance, with no help. A magnetic material that is NOT itself a magnet can only get pulled TOWARD a magnet — it cannot then go and pull other magnetic objects toward itself.

🧲
IS a magnet
can pull other magnetic objects toward itself, all on its own — e.g. a fridge magnet, a bar magnet, a compass needle
🔩
Just a magnetic MATERIAL (not a magnet)
gets pulled by a magnet, but cannot pull other magnetic objects on its own — e.g. a plain steel paperclip, a plain iron nail
The test in one sentence: does it attract OTHER magnetic objects, all by itself, with no magnet nearby? If yes, it's a magnet. If it only ever gets pulled — but never does the pulling itself — it's just made of a magnetic material.

Magnet or Magnetic Material? Decision Tree 🌳

Does the object attract OTHER magnetic objects all by itself, with no magnet nearby to help it?
YES — it can pull other magnetic things toward itself, unaided
It IS a magnet
NO — it only ever gets pulled TOWARD a magnet, never does the pulling
Just a magnetic material
Worked example: a thin sheet of metal X is held near a magnetized bar and gets pulled toward it. X is then held near a plain, unmagnetized iron nail — and nothing happens. What does this tell us about X? Since X was pulled BY the magnetized bar but could NOT pull the plain iron nail, X must be a magnetic material (like steel) — but X is NOT a magnet itself. If X were a real magnet, it would have pulled the plain iron nail toward it too, with no help needed.

2. Which of these is an INSULATOR, not a conductor?

✔ Correct — dry wood is an insulator; it blocks electricity from flowing through it.
✘ Re-check Screen 5 — most metals are conductors; wood, plastic, rubber, and glass are insulators.

3. A student rubs a plastic ruler on their hair. The ruler gains extra electrons. What charge does the ruler now have, and what will happen if it's brought near tiny bits of paper?

✔ Correct — gaining electrons makes the ruler negatively charged, and a charged object always attracts neutral objects through induction.
✘ Re-check Screen 6 — GAINING electrons means NEGATIVE charge, and a charged object attracts anything neutral nearby.

4. A steel spoon is placed near a strong bar magnet and gets pulled toward it. The same spoon is then held near a plain steel fork with no magnet involved. Nothing happens. What does this tell us about the spoon?

✔ Correct — the spoon gets pulled by a real magnet, but cannot pull other magnetic objects on its own, so it's a magnetic material, not a magnet.
✘ Re-check Screens 7-8 — being ATTRACTED to a magnet is not the same as BEING a magnet. The test is whether it can pull other magnetic objects unaided.

5. Which of these objects IS a true magnet, not just a magnetic material?

✔ Correct — a compass needle is a tiny magnet itself; that's exactly why it can line up with Earth's magnetic field and point North on its own.
✘ Re-check Screens 7-8 — a plain iron nail and steel paperclip are only magnetic MATERIALS; aluminium isn't magnetic at all. Only the compass needle is an actual magnet.
