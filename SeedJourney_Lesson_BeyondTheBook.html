Built to Travel

Gardeners guess how far a seed will go just by looking at its shape.

Wind carries light seeds with wings or hairs a long way. A big, heavy seed has nothing to catch the wind, so it falls close to the parent. Drop each seed in the same steady wind.

The lighter the seed, and the more wing or hair it has, the farther it goes.
Remember: light seeds with wings or hairs travel far. A heavy acorn drops near the tree.

Why Fruits Are Tasty

Sweet, colourful fruit is an advert. It tells animals: eat me. Hooks cling to the fur instead, but a tasty fruit is carried inside the animal. Both are seed dispersal.

Animals eat the fruit and drop the seeds somewhere new, far from the parent. So animals eat the fruit and drop the seeds for the plant, and the animal gets a meal.

Recap from Unit 62: cross-pollination mixes the parents, so the young plants differ from each other.

A bird eats a ripe berry and flies off. Later it drops the seed. Where is the seed now?

The Starch Test

Food scientists test foods for starch. Starch is a food store inside many plants and seeds.

The Membrane Tube

A membrane is a thin skin with tiny holes. Cells have one, and so does the tube here. Tiny pieces pass through. Big pieces cannot. The rule: iodine can pass through, starch cannot.
iodine, smallstarch, big

A thin-skinned tube holds white starch mixture. It stands in a glass of brown iodine. Predict first: what will you see?

Pick your prediction.
Result: the inside turns blue-black. The outside stays brown. Iodine can pass through, starch cannot. The colour shows where the STARCH is.
At school, one answer said the liquid outside would turn dark blue. But starch cannot get out, so it cannot colour the outside.

Per-N: Counting the Ones That Grow

“1 in every 100” means: for each group of 100, one seed grows. To find how many grow, divide by the group size. Watch one example, then do your own.

Seeds that grow

Watch me
Step 1. A vine drops 300 seeds. Only 1 in every 100 grows. That is a small part of 300, so the answer is smaller than 300.
Step 2. 1 in every 100 means groups of 100. To count the groups, divide by 100.
Step 3. 300 ÷ 100 = 3. About 3 seeds grow.
Step 4. Size check: 3 is smaller than 300. It fits. A wrong move like 300 × 100 gives a number far bigger than the seeds there are.
