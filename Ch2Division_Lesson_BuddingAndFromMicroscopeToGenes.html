One Cell Makes a Crowd

Bread rises because of a living thing called yeast. One yeast cell can turn into millions in a day. Today you see how a yeast cell makes a new cell, how fast the numbers grow, and how scientists zoom into a cell to find the instructions inside.
Recall: yeast is a fungus made of just one cell, so it is unicellular. You met it in Unit 4, making bread dough rise.

Budding: A New Cell Grows Out

Yeast, a fungus, makes new cells by budding. It is the dividing slice of the yeast's cell cycle. Press the button to watch one bud.
1234
A yeast cell, with its nucleus. We draw 2 chromosomes to keep it clear. A small bump grows on the side. In the nucleus, each chromosome makes a copy. One copy of every chromosome moves into the bump. The other copy stays. The bud pinches off. Now there are two cells, and each has all the chromosomes.

Budding Makes an Identical Cell

Budding is asexual: one parent, chromosomes copied exactly. So the new cell is identical to its parent, and it starts its own cell cycle: grow, then bud. It is the same rule as runners and cuttings in Unit 46.

The Same Thing, in Steps (Route B)

The animation showed it. Now the steps, so you can work out any number.
Example: a yeast cell buds every 3 hours. How many cells after 6 hours?
  1. Find the gap: 3 hours between budding rounds. The gap tells you how long one round takes.
  2. Rounds = total time ÷ gap: 6 hours ÷ 3 hours = 2 rounds. Dividing counts how many gaps fit in the time.
  3. Start at 1 and double once per round: 1 → 2 → 4. Each round, every cell buds.
  4. Sense-check: does 4 make sense? Doubling grows fast, but it cannot make cells from nothing, and it cannot give 6 just because there were 6 hours.
Answer: 4 cells. Gap first, then rounds, then doubling.

What Is a Gene?

A gene is an instruction for one feature, such as eye colour. A chromosome carries many genes, like a recipe book with many recipes. In each cell cycle, a cell copies its chromosomes, and so copies every gene.
Children get chromosomes from both parents, so they get a mix of instructions. That is why a child can look a bit like each parent. You will learn more in Chapter 3.
