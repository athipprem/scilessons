Recap: The 8 Planets 🪐

From Unit 43: the Solar System has 8 planets in two families — 4 small ROCKY planets close to the Sun (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and 4 giant GAS/ICE planets further out (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune) — with the ASTEROID BELT lying between Mars and Jupiter.
But the asteroid belt isn't empty rock and dust for no reason — those leftover pieces are clues to how the whole Solar System formed. Today you'll meet the other objects that share the Solar System with the 8 planets.

Dwarf Planets 🌑

A DWARF PLANET orbits the Sun and is round (its own gravity pulled it into a ball) — but unlike a full planet, it has NOT cleared other objects out of the path of its orbit.

Key idea: Pluto was called the 9th planet until 2006, when scientists agreed on this clearer rule and reclassified it as a dwarf planet. Other dwarf planets include Ceres (the largest object in the asteroid belt) and Eris.

Asteroids, Comets & Meteors ☄️

These small space objects are often confused with each other — but each has its own clear definition.
ASTEROID: a rocky (or metallic) lump of leftover material orbiting the Sun, mostly found in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.
COMET: a ball of ice, dust, and rock that orbits the Sun on a long, stretched-out path. As it nears the Sun, the ice heats up and releases gas and dust, forming a glowing tail that always points AWAY from the Sun.
METEOROID → METEOR → METEORITE (one object, three names as it travels): a small rock drifting through space is a METEOROID. If it burns up entering Earth's atmosphere, the streak of light you see is a METEOR ("shooting star"). If a piece survives and lands on the ground, that surviving piece is a METEORITE.

Key idea: the name changes based on WHERE the object is and what's happening to it — not what it's fundamentally made of. A meteoroid, meteor, and meteorite can all be the exact same rock at three different moments in its journey.

Satellites, Probes & Rovers 🛰️

Recap from Unit 43: a SATELLITE is anything that orbits a planet — natural (like the Moon) or man-made. But "exploring space" also uses two other kinds of machines.
🛰️ Satellite
Orbits Earth (or another planet). Examples: the ISS (International Space Station), the Hubble Space Telescope, GPS satellites.
🚀 Probe
Travels far into space to fly past, orbit, or study a planet/moon/comet WITHOUT landing and returning. Example: the Voyager probes, now beyond our Solar System.
🤖 Rover: a wheeled robot that actually LANDS on another world's surface and drives around collecting samples and data. Example: the Perseverance and Curiosity rovers exploring Mars.
Key idea: the difference is WHERE it ends up and what it does there — a satellite stays in orbit, a probe flies by or orbits without landing, and a rover lands and moves around on the surface itself.
