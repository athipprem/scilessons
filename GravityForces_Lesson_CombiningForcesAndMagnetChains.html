Quick Recall: Add or Cancel? 🔁

🔁 Remember Unit 29/30? Forces in the SAME direction ADD fully. Forces in OPPOSITE directions SUBTRACT — if they're equal, they cancel completely (balanced); if unequal, the leftover (bigger minus smaller) pushes the object toward the stronger side. 🔁 Remember Unit 34/35? A chain of touching magnets is read ONE JOIN at a time: matching poles (N–N or S–S) repel, different poles (N–S) attract — and every single magnet always has a North end AND a South end, never just one.

Today both ideas come back — but this time, with THREE forces at once instead of two, and a magnet chain where one magnet gets physically turned around partway through.

Why learn this? 🤔

On the real ASMOPSS 2026 exam, Prem correctly solved a 2-magnet chain question — but missed an almost identical chain that had ONE EXTRA STEP: one magnet had been physically flipped around. Same rule, same underlying skill, but one extra thing to track broke it. This is exactly what working-memory stress-testing is for.
📋 The real finding: Q18 (a straightforward magnet chain, no flip) was answered correctly, with matching working shown. Q33 (the same kind of chain, but with a flip added) was missed. The magnet-pole rule itself wasn't the problem — tracking one extra operation on top of it was.
Real life: engineers assembling machines with multiple magnetic parts, or technicians wiring circuits with several components, constantly have to track multiple changes at once without losing their place — exactly the skill this lesson builds.

Three Forces, One at a Time ➕➕➕

With 3+ forces, don't try to combine them all in your head at once — group SAME-direction forces together FIRST, then compare the two totals.
A crate has three forces acting on it: 12N and 8N both pushing left, and 15N pushing right.
Method, step by step: (1) Sort every force into two piles by direction — left-pushers and right-pushers. (2) Add up each pile separately: left pile = 12+8 = 20N. Right pile = 15N (only one force). (3) Compare the two totals like a simple tug-of-war: 20N − 15N = 5N net force, toward the bigger pile's direction (left).

Magnet Chains WITH a Flip 🔄

A "flip" means one magnet in the chain gets physically turned end-for-end AFTER you've already worked out its poles. Once it's flipped, that magnet's North and South ends SWAP PLACES — everything past that point must be re-checked.
Before the flip, X (N–S) and Y (N–S) touch S-to-N, so they attract. After Y is physically flipped end-for-end, Y's poles swap places — now X's S touches Y's S, so the SAME join now repels instead.
The one rule that fixes this every time: a flip does NOT change the rule (same poles still repel, different poles still attract) — it only changes WHICH pole is facing which way. Treat the touching join AFTER a flip as a brand-new join, and re-apply the rule fresh, exactly like Screen 2's reminder that this is the same skill you already know — just one more thing to track.

Handling a Multi-Step Chain 🌳

Does the problem mention a magnet being flipped, turned around, or reversed partway through?
↓
NO flip mentioned
Work the chain join by join as usual, start to finish
YES, a flip happens
Work the chain up to the flip normally, THEN treat every join touching the flipped magnet as brand new — re-derive from scratch, don't assume the old answer still holds
Same idea applies to forces: if a question adds a NEW force partway through a scenario (a third person joins the tug-of-war, a second engine turns on), don't just adjust your old total by guessing — redo the "sort into piles, then compare" method fresh with all the forces now in play.

Worked Example: Three Magnets, One Flip 🧩

Scenario: Magnets P, Q, and R touch in a row: P–Q, then Q–R. P's left end is S. The P–Q join attracts. The Q–R join repels. THEN, magnet R gets physically flipped around.
Step 1: P's left is S (given), so P's right (touching Q) must be N (every magnet has two opposite poles).
Step 2: P–Q join attracts, meaning the two touching ends are DIFFERENT poles. P's touching end is N, so Q's left end (touching P) must be S.
Step 3: Q has two opposite poles — its left is S, so Q's right end (touching R) must be N.
Step 4: Q–R join repels (BEFORE the flip), meaning the two touching ends are the SAME pole. Q's touching end is N, so R's left end (touching Q) must also be N — and R's other end must be S.
Step 5 — the flip: R is physically turned around. R's poles SWAP places: what was R's left end (N) is now on the right, and what was R's right end (S) is now on the left, touching Q.
Step 6 — re-check the Q–R join fresh: Q's touching end is still N (nothing about Q changed). R's NEW touching end (after the flip) is S. Different poles touching now → the Q–R join now ATTRACTS, the opposite of before the flip.
Final answer: before the flip, Q–R repelled. After flipping R, Q–R attracts instead — even though the underlying rule (same poles repel, different poles attract) never changed at all.

Your Turn — Check Yourself ✅

Pick an answer, then press Check. Once you check, that answer is locked in — so choose carefully, and track each step slowly!

1. A crate has three forces acting on it: 10N left, 6N left, and 9N right. What is the net force, and which way does the crate move?

✔ Correct — left pile: 10+6=16N. Right pile: 9N. 16−9=7N net, toward the bigger pile (left).
✘ Re-check Screen 4 — group the same-direction forces into piles first, then compare the two totals.

2. Magnets A and B touch. A's left end is N. The A–B join attracts. Then B is physically flipped around. What happens to the A–B join AFTER the flip?

✔ Correct — A's left is N, so A's right (touching B) is S. Attracting means B's original touching end was N. After B is flipped, B's touching end becomes S instead — same poles (S–S) now touch, so it repels.
✘ Re-check Screen 5 — what does a flip actually do to a magnet's poles, and why does that mean the join must be re-checked?

3. A tug-of-war has two teams pulling with 18N and 18N in opposite directions — balanced. A THIRD person joins the 18N team, adding 7N more force on that same side. What happens now?

✔ Correct — re-total that side fresh (18+7=25N) and compare to the other side (18N) — 25 beats 18, so the rope moves toward the reinforced side.
✘ Re-check Screen 6 — when a new force joins partway through, should you guess the new result or redo the "sort and compare" method fresh?

4. Magnets P, Q, R touch in a row. Before any flip, the Q–R join repels. Someone flips R around. What is now true about the P–Q join, which is far away from R?

✔ Correct — only the join(s) actually touching the flipped magnet can change; P and Q never moved, so their join stays exactly as it was.
✘ Re-check Screen 7 — a flip only swaps the poles of the ONE magnet that moved. Which join does that actually affect?

5. A student claims: "Once you flip any magnet in a chain, the whole chain's rule changes completely — same poles start attracting instead." What's wrong with this claim?

✔ Correct — the underlying rule is permanent; a flip only changes which end is facing which direction, not the rule itself. This is the exact overgeneralization trap from Trap-Recognition Drill #2.
✘ Re-check Screen 5 — does a flip change the RULE, or just which pole happens to be facing which way?
