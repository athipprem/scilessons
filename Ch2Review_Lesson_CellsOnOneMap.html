Part 2: Real Size — what does it mean?

A microscope makes things look bigger. “×20” means the picture is 20 times bigger than the real thing. To get back to the real size, we undo the bigger: we divide.
Calc check every time: 1. Say what the number means. 2. Write the step. 3. Size check.

👀 Watch me

An ant’s leg is photographed with a ×10 eyepiece and a ×2 objective. In the photo the leg is 40 mm long. How long is the real leg?
1Find the total magnification. Eyepiece × objective = 10 × 2 = 20. The photo is 20 times bigger than the real leg.
2Bigger or smaller? The real leg is smaller than the photo. Smaller means divide.
3Write the sum. Photo size ÷ total magnification = 40 ÷ 20.
4Work it out. 40 ÷ 20 = 2. The real leg is 2 mm long.
5Size check. Is 2 mm smaller than 40 mm? Yes. Check back: 2 × 20 = 40, the same as the photo. The answer is right.

Part 2: How many cells after some time?

The rule: Rounds = time ÷ gap. Count the rounds first, then double once for each round. Calc check: say what the numbers mean, write each step, size check.

👀 Watch me

A cell divides every 30 minutes. How many cells are there after 2 hours?
1Use the same units. The gap is in minutes, so change hours to minutes: 2 hours = 60 + 60 = 120 minutes.
2Count the rounds. Rounds = time ÷ gap = 120 ÷ 30 = 4 rounds.
3Double once per round. Start at 1: 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16. Four arrows for four rounds. Answer: 16 cells.
4Size check. Count the arrows: 4, the same as the number of rounds. Each number is double the one before. It passes.

Cells in 12 Lines

The same map, one line at a time.
  1. The cell is the basic unit of all living things.
  2. Unicellular: made of one cell (amoeba, bacteria, yeast). Multicellular: made of many cells.
  3. The nucleus controls the cell.
  4. The cell membrane controls what moves in and out.
  5. The cytoplasm is the jelly where the work happens.
  6. Plant cells also have a cell wall (made of cellulose), a large vacuole (holds cell sap; when it is full the plant cell is firm, or turgid) and chloroplasts (make food using light).
  7. Animal cells have none of those three.
  8. Total magnification = eyepiece × objective. Start on low power.
  9. Levels: cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism.
  10. A nerve cell carries messages. A root hair cell has more surface to take in water. A red blood cell carries oxygen. A muscle cell can shorten.
  11. Cells divide: one cell becomes two identical cells, for growth and repair.
  12. Each round the number of cells doubles: 1, 2, 4, 8. Count the rounds first. A bud pinches off a yeast cell. Osmosis: water moves into a cell from where there is more water to where there is less.

Activity Corner

Making a slide: use a thin piece of specimen, add a drop of stain, then lower the coverslip at an angle so no bubbles are trapped.
Budding: a bump grows on the yeast cell, then the bud pinches off and becomes a new cell.
Osmosis: water moves into a cell from where there is more water to where there is less. A cell that gains water swells.
